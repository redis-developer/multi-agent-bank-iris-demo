# EMI and Interest Calculation Guide

product: general

## How EMI is calculated

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) uses the reducing-balance formula:
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the principal, r is the
monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the tenure in months.
Example: ₹5,00,000 at 11.5% for 48 months gives an EMI of about ₹13,044. Each
EMI pays that month's interest first; the remainder reduces principal, so the
interest portion falls every month.

## Reducing balance vs flat rate

Our loans use reducing-balance interest: interest accrues only on the
outstanding principal. A "flat rate" quote (interest on the full principal for
the whole tenure) looks cheaper but is roughly 1.8x the equivalent reducing
rate — a 7% flat rate costs about the same as 12.5% reducing. Always compare
loans on the reducing-balance APR.

## Total interest and amortisation

Total interest = EMI × n − P. For the example above: 13,044 × 48 − 5,00,000 ≈
₹1,26,112. The amortisation schedule shows the principal/interest split per
month. Part-prepayments reduce the outstanding principal immediately, which
either shortens the tenure (keeping the EMI) or lowers the EMI (keeping the
tenure) — shortening the tenure saves more interest.

## What changes your EMI

Your EMI changes when: the rate resets (floating-rate loans), you make a
part-prepayment and opt for EMI reduction, or you restructure the tenure. A
missed EMI does not change the EMI amount but adds late charges and interest
on the overdue amount, and is reported to the credit bureau after 30 days.
